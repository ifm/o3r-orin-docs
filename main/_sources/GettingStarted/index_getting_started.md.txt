# Getting started with the O3R

This guide walks you through setting up the O3R platform, from unboxing and wiring the hardware to choosing the software stack you will develop with.

## Hardware unboxing

### Before you start

Prepare the following O3R hardware:

````{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 25 25 50

* - Hardware
   - Image
   - Requirement
* - OVP93x VPU
   - :::{image} ../resources/OVP930AA.jpg
      :alt: OVP930 VPU with power, Ethernet, and camera connectors
      :width: 160px
      :::
   - One VPU, such as the OVP930 or OVP931.
      See the [available models and application licenses](../AvailableHardware/hardware_specifications.md#hardware-list).
* - O3R335 camera heads
   - :::{image} ../resources/O3R355AA.jpg
      :alt: O3R camera head with front windows and a side cable connector
      :width: 160px
      :::
   - One or more heads, depending on the camera streams required.
      The VPU has four camera ports; a head using both 2D and 3D occupies two ports.
* - High-speed FAKRA Mini (HFM) cables
   - :::{image} ../resources/E3R100.jpg
      :alt: E3R100 dual HFM cable with one camera connector and two VPU connectors
      :width: 160px
      :::
   - One suitable cable assembly per head.
      Use a dual connection for 2D and 3D, or a single connection for 3D only.
      The image shows the E3R100 dual cable.
````

Also prepare the following power and network accessories:

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Power supply | A reliable power supply capable of providing at least 24 V / 5 A. Follow the [power-supply guidance](../IntegrationGuides/Electrical/electrical_guide.md#power-supply-and-consumption). |
| Power/CAN cable | An M12 connector cable for the VPU, such as EVC948. EVC706 is a non-CAN alternative. See the [cable catalog](../AvailableHardware/hardware_specifications.md#hardware-list). |
| Ethernet cable | A cable rated for 1 Gbit/s (Gigabit) communication. |

#### Power consumption

With maximum CPU/GPU load and four 3D heads at 20 fps, the system consumes up to 75 W / 3100 mA RMS in standard mode or 94 W / 3900 mA RMS in super mode.
For example, in standard mode:

- A VPU with one 3D head consumes 35 W / 1500 mA.
- An ODS application with four 3D heads consumes 67 W / 2800 mA for the VPU and heads combined.

These are consumption figures, not power-supply ratings.
See the [power consumption tables and examples](../IntegrationGuides/Electrical/electrical_guide.md#power-supply-and-consumption) for the other configurations.
See the [mechanical integration guide](../IntegrationGuides/Mechanical/mechanical_guide.md#super-mode-heat-dissipation) for heat-dissipation requirements.

### Connect the hardware

Before wiring the platform, review the [O3R335 clearance definition and illustrations](../IntegrationGuides/Mechanical/mechanical_guide.md#o3r335) and the [mounting examples](../IntegrationGuides/Mechanical/mechanical_guide.md#examples) when positioning the cameras.

Watch the wiring video or follow the steps below:

:::{youtube} msPANzaOuSY
:::

Disconnect the power supply before making electrical connections.

1. First, connect the heads to the VPU, as shown below:  
   ![Two O3R335 camera heads connected to the four OVP93x camera ports, with Ethernet and power connections](./resources/connection_overview.drawio.svg "Connections")  

   Some example scenarios:

   The diagram shows example 1. Any camera port can carry a 2D or 3D stream; adjacent ports do not need to use the same imager type.

   | Connection    | Port 0        | Port 1        | Port 2        | Port 3        |
   | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
   | **example 1** | camera 1 (2D) | camera 1 (3D) | camera 2 (2D) | camera 2 (3D) |
   | **example 2** | camera 1 (2D) | camera 2 (2D) | camera 1 (3D) | camera 2 (3D) |
   | **example 3** | camera 1 (3D) | camera 2 (3D) | camera 3 (3D) | camera 4 (3D) |

2. Connect power to the VPU, the pins are defined as follows:
   - 1 screen
   - 2 24 V
   - 3 GND
   - 4 CAN +
   - 5 CAN -
3. Connect the Ethernet cable,
4. Once all the connected cameras LEDs are green, the VPU is properly booted up.  

That's it!
